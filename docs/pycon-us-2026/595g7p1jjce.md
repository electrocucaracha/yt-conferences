# Summary

Armen Martirossian argues that most technical project failures stem from sociological issues rather than technological ones, emphasizing that hidden evaluations in code reviews often trigger defensiveness and derail productive discussions.
He advocates for using "I statements" to express personal confusion or concerns, which shifts the dynamic from implicit blame to explicit ownership of one’s own problems, thereby reducing the likelihood of the other party raising a "defensiveness exception." Martirossian explains that approaching conversations with curiosity and warmth, rather than an attempt to prove one is right, prevents emotional flooding and keeps the dialogue open for genuine collaboration.
He also highlights the "average familiarity" trap, where experts overestimate others' knowledge in their specific area of strength, suggesting that recognizing this cognitive bias helps reframe frustration into constructive, respectful communication.
Ultimately, the talk encourages developers to treat mental reframing like code refactoring, continuously adjusting their communication strategies to foster autonomy, mutual respect, and better technical outcomes.
