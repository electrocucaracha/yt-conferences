# Summary

The speaker, an Afro-Colombian product manager from Cali, presents "Python Schools," an initiative designed to promote social mobility among underrepresented communities by teaching Python programming to youth aged 15 to 20 in the marginalized eastern district of Aguablanca.
This area, historically settled by Afro-descendants migrating from the Pacific coast, faces significant structural inequality, poverty, and state neglect, which the program aims to counteract by providing a safe, Afro-centered space where students can see themselves represented in the tech industry.
The curriculum focuses on Python and Raspberry Pi because of their accessibility, while the program also addresses critical barriers to participation by guaranteeing transportation, lunch, and snacks, ensuring that economic constraints do not prevent attendance.
By empowering students to become creators of technology rather than just consumers, the initiative seeks to instill pride and confidence, addressing the broader lack of Black representation in software development roles in Colombia.
The project, currently in its second year, is funded by the Colombian Ministry of Technology and has successfully retained students who initially joined for the food but now remain motivated by the educational opportunities and community support.
