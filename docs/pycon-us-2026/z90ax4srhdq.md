# Summary

Amanda Casari opens her talk by acknowledging the challenges facing the global Python community, emphasizing the importance of presence and community building despite current geopolitical and social tensions.
She uses the Apollo guidance computer and the Artemis project as metaphors to argue that while automation is advancing, human agency and autonomy remain critical for complex decision-making, distinguishing between automation, autonomy, and agency.
Casari critiques the current conflation of these concepts in AI and open source, warning that selling agents as a replacement for human labor ignores the essential need for human oversight, maintenance, and ethical governance.
She advocates for setting healthy boundaries, recognizing that open source labor is not free, and prioritizing well-being to move from a state of scarcity to spaciousness.
Ultimately, she defines hope as the collective belief that through intentional community work, empathy, and the defense of human agency, we can build a sustainable future where technology serves humanity rather than replacing it.
