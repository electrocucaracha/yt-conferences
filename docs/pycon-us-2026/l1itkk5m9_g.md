# Summary

Joaquín Matres, a former engineer at Google and HP, presents GDS Factory, a Python-based library designed to make chip design tools more accessible and intuitive compared to traditional, expensive software.
The tool, built on the KLayout C++ engine, is 200 times faster than legacy systems and supports the design of various technologies, including photonics and quantum computing, by leveraging the extensive Python scientific ecosystem.
Matres demonstrates how the library allows users to easily draw, simulate, and optimize quantum chips, such as photonic qubits, which offer advantages in scalability and reduced interaction errors compared to superconducting qubits.
The company operates on an open-source model, providing free access to over 10 libraries and supporting 43 foundries, while generating revenue through private library support and services for enterprises and universities.
The discussion further explores the future of quantum computing, noting that QPUs will likely coexist with CPUs and GPUs to handle specific optimization and probabilistic tasks, such as molecular modeling and AI improvements, rather than replacing classical computers entirely.
Matres emphasizes that by lowering the barrier to entry for hardware design, GDS Factory aims to create a collaborative, open-source ecosystem where anyone can design chips, similar to how Linux democratized software development.
