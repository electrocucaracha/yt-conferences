# Summary

The Python Software Foundation (PSF) reaffirmed its mission to promote the Python language and support a diverse global community, emphasizing that open source development is essential for building a collaborative future rather than a corporate-dominated one.
A significant highlight of the presentation was the PSF’s decision to unanimously turn down a $1.5 million grant from the National Science Foundation due to restrictive "no DEI" contract language, a stance that was strongly supported by the community, which subsequently raised nearly $600,000 from over 5,500 donors to fund security work, PyPI maintenance, and community initiatives.
The organization also expressed gratitude to major corporate sponsors, including Anthropic, Meta, and Google, who provide critical funding for CPython developers and security roles.
The PSF team, consisting of just 11 staff members, was recognized for their year-round dedication to managing infrastructure, supporting fiscal sponsors like PyLadies, and organizing PyCon US.
Finally, the event concluded with the presentation of PSF Community Service Awards to individuals such as Jay Miller and Sarah Kuchinsky, as well as Outstanding PyLadies Awards to four women who have made meaningful contributions to leadership, mentorship, and inclusive community building within the Python ecosystem.
