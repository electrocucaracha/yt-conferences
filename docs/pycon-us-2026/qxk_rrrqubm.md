# Summary

Bay, a data scientist and organizer for PyLadies Boston, presents a walkability project focused on Malden, Massachusetts, driven by concerns over pedestrian safety and fatal car crashes.
Utilizing data from the Massachusetts Department of Transportation, Bay analyzed crash locations, severity, and vulnerable user involvement to challenge the conventional "Walk Score" metric, which often overlooks safety in favor of proximity to amenities.
To address this gap, Bay developed an application called MOSEY that integrates crash data with walk scores to provide a more comprehensive view of neighborhood safety.
Collaborating with the Malden Safe Streets group, the project advocates for infrastructure improvements such as protected multi-use trails and conducts detailed walk audits to assess sidewalk conditions and crossing safety.
The presentation highlights successful interventions, such as replacing a rotary with a traffic signal that reduced crashes, and outlines future goals including analyzing national trends, processing police reports, and expanding data collection to better understand how local changes impact overall walkability and community safety.
