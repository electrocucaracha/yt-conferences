# Summary

Valentina Lázaro, a systems engineer from Bolivia, presented a technology-driven project designed to detect early signs of depression in university students, addressing the high prevalence of mental health disorders in Latin America and the stigma that often prevents students from seeking help.
The system utilizes Python for data analysis and Natural Language Processing, specifically fine-tuning the Beto model to interpret open-ended emotional responses, alongside structured data collection to identify risk factors related to academic, economic, and social pressures.
By integrating these insights into a mobile application for students and a web portal for psychology departments, the tool generates alerts for moderate to high-risk cases while maintaining strict data anonymity and encryption until a professional confirms the need for intervention.
Lázaro emphasized that the system is intended to complement, not replace, clinical judgment, achieving 89% precision in its assessments and demonstrating a scalable framework that can be adapted for other institutions or disorders, with plans to make the project open source to maximize its social impact.
