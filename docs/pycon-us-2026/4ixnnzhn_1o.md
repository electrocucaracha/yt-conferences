# Summary

Elaine, the outgoing chair of PyCon US, delivered closing remarks that highlighted the conference’s success, noting 2,003 attendees, $60,000 raised for PyLadies, and the sold-out status of t-shirts.
She expressed gratitude to the volunteers, sponsors, and staff for making the event in Long Beach possible, particularly acknowledging the challenges of hosting in a new city and introducing new tracks for AI, security, and Spanish-language keynotes.
Elaine officially passed the chair role to John Bonafarto, who announced that PyCon US 2027 will return to Long Beach from May 12 to 18.
Following the chair transition, various representatives from around the world promoted upcoming Python conferences, including PyCon Italia, EuroPython, SciPy, PyOhio, and events in Asia, Latin America, and Europe.
Finally, Chuck introduced the sprint sessions scheduled for the following two days, encouraging attendees to participate in open-source contributions for projects such as Django, CPython, Mesa, and BeeWare, with specific guidance provided for beginners and details on where to find project leads.
