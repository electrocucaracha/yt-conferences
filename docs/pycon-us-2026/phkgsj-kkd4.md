# Summary

Mike Cuthbert, a Python user and former MIT professor, presents a talk at PyCon exploring the bidirectional relationship between computer science and the arts, specifically music.
He argues that while applied computation often uses CS to analyze other fields, engaging with arts and humanities can also reinforce fundamental programming skills, particularly for students from underrepresented backgrounds who find traditional CS applications less resonant.
Cuthbert introduces Music21, a Python library he created to facilitate symbolic music analysis and composition, demonstrating how it allows users to manipulate musical notes, chords, and structures with simple code.
He illustrates how computational music theory helps students grasp complex CS concepts such as recursion, graph theory, and data representation biases by applying them to tangible musical problems.
Finally, he shares transferable patterns from his digital humanities lab, such as the "code and then unlock" paradigm, encouraging developers to leverage their personal passions to learn programming and highlighting the importance of inclusive, culturally aware data representation in computational tools.
