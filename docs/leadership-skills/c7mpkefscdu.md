# Summary

Doug Howard, a licensed structural engineer and former engineering manager, explains that technical skills and high IQ are no longer the primary factors determining success for engineers.
He describes how advancements in technology, automation, and design software have diminished the value of highly specialized roles, making technical skills a basic prerequisite rather than a differentiator.
Instead, engineering managers now prioritize soft skills such as communication, teamwork, adaptability, professionalism, and the ability to handle criticism and stress.
Howard emphasizes that the most successful engineers are those who can lead cross-functional teams, influence others, and solve problems collaboratively, making people skills far more valuable than technical expertise in today’s rapidly evolving engineering landscape.
