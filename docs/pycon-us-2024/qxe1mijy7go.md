# Summary

Marata, the PyCon US conference chair, and Christian, the co-chair of the PyCon Charlas track, discuss the open Call for Proposals (CFP) for the Spanish-language track, which remains open until December 16.
PyCon Charlas is a special initiative designed to foster inclusion and diversity by allowing speakers to present in Spanish, a language in which many attendees feel more comfortable and authentic.
Christian explains that the track originated from a desire to correct name pronunciation errors and promote broader linguistic representation, and it now features fully Spanish-language talks, MCs, and announcements.
For submissions, speakers are advised to use the specific Spanish-language section on the PyCon US site to submit proposals via PreTalks, ensuring they include a compelling abstract, a detailed description, and a structured outline with time estimates.
Christian emphasizes that a well-structured proposal is crucial for acceptance, noting that reviewers look for clear context, problem definition, and a logical flow, while also encouraging speakers to share personal stories and unique experiences rather than only focusing on the latest technical trends.
The track typically offers 30-minute slots, and the program chairs work to create a supportive environment by allowing speakers to choose their preferred Q&A format and educating the audience to provide respectful feedback.
Regarding logistics, speakers do not receive direct payment, but the Python Software Foundation (PSF) offers travel and accommodation grants to help offset costs, which Marata encourages attendees to apply for regardless of whether their talk is accepted.
The review process is largely anonymous, and acceptance is based on the quality of the proposal rather than the speaker’s fame.
Finally, Marata highlights other ways to participate in PyCon US, such as lightning talks, summits, and sponsor presentations, reinforcing the community’s commitment to making the conference accessible and inclusive for all.
