# Summary

Jay Miller delivers a keynote addressing the lack of representation and belonging experienced by Black developers in the Python community, citing his own experience of seeing only twelve Black attendees at PyCon US 2022.
He argues that for many, the effort to attend major conferences is not worth the lack of connection, leading to a perception that the community is not inclusive or safe.
In response, Miller and his colleagues launched "Black Python Devs," a nonprofit organization supported by the Gnome Foundation, which aims to create a safe, equitable, and accessible space for Black developers to connect, lead, and thrive.
The organization has already grown to over 400 members globally, secured infrastructure sponsorship from JetBrains, and successfully tripled Black attendance at PyCon 2023 by fostering genuine community bonds rather than just diversity metrics.
Miller concludes by urging the broader Python community to support this initiative through donations, open-source contributions, and active engagement, emphasizing that building a more inclusive environment benefits everyone and ensures that Black developers can find their "Wakanda" within the Python ecosystem.
