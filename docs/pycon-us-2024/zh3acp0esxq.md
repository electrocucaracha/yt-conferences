# Summary

Carlos Aranibar, a neuroimaging specialist from Bolivia currently based in Chicago, presented a talk on facilitating the comprehension of EEG analysis.
He explained that while MRI and CT scans are used for structural imaging, EEG measures the brain's electrical activity through non-invasive sensors placed on the scalp, capturing different wave patterns like delta, theta, alpha, beta, and gamma that correlate with various states of consciousness and alertness.
Aranibar highlighted the challenges patients face in interpreting standard EEG line graphs and introduced the open-source Python library MNE, which supports advanced data processing techniques such as Independent Component Analysis (ICA) to isolate specific brain activities and filter out noise.
He demonstrated how MNE can visualize data through 3D topographic maps and heatmaps, making it easier to identify abnormal activities such as epileptic seizures or responses to visual stimuli.
Furthermore, he discussed the potential of consumer-grade devices like the Muse headband for continuous, personalized monitoring, arguing that frequent, low-cost at-home data collection can complement traditional hospital tests by providing a more accurate long-term baseline of an individual's brain activity.
