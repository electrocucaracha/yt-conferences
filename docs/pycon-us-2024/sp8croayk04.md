# Summary

Kelsey Wo, a senior software engineer and knitter, presents a talk at PyCon US that draws parallels between programming and fiber arts, arguing that knitting patterns function as "programs for your hands." She highlights the historical influence of weaving on early computing, citing the Jacquard loom’s punch cards and the Apollo Guidance Computer’s core rope memory as key examples of how textile mechanics inspired digital technology.
Wo further maps specific programming concepts to knitting techniques, such as treating basic stitches as language keywords, defined stitch patterns as functions, and size variations as control flow statements, using her experience creating a custom Minecraft skeleton plushie to demonstrate this practical application.
The talk also emphasizes the strong cultural alignment between the Python and fiber arts communities, particularly their shared commitment to diversity, inclusion, and accessibility, which inspired Wo to pursue a career in software engineering.
She concludes by encouraging developers to explore these cross-disciplinary connections to gain new perspectives on problem-solving, debugging, and collaboration, while celebrating the physical satisfaction and community support found in crafting.
