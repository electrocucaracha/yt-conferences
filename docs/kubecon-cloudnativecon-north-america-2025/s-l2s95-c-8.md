# Summary

The transcript discusses the evolving challenges of scheduling large, multi-host AI workloads in Kubernetes as AI models and their hardware requirements grow.
Traditional Kubernetes scheduling, designed for single-host workloads, is insufficient for modern AI tasks that require coordinated placement of pods across groups of nodes connected by specialized network fabrics like NVLink or Infiniband.
To address this, the Kubernetes community has leveraged extensibility features, creating custom workload controllers and alternative schedulers, but this has led to fragmentation and inconsistent APIs.
The core team is now working to standardize support for multi-host workloads by introducing pod groups, gang scheduling, and topology-aware placement, aiming for more global, workload-aware scheduling decisions.
Future plans include tighter integration with infrastructure autoscaling, improved extensibility for third-party schedulers, and mechanisms for resource reservation, all while maintaining flexibility for innovation and feedback from the community.
