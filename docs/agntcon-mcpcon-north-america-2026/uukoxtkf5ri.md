# Summary

Dakota Fabro, a software engineer at Block, presents a framework for evaluating AI collaboration that shifts focus from measuring tools to assessing the human user’s cognitive engagement.
Drawing on backgrounds in psychology, theology, and education, Fabro argues that current AI maturity models fail to account for how AI use shapes individual creativity and cognition.
The proposed framework, known as RPY, utilizes three dimensions: Depth of Knowledge (DOK) to measure the complexity of thinking, tool maturity to assess the alignment between user skill and AI capabilities, and agentic delegation trust to evaluate the verification and evidence-based management of AI outputs.
Fabro emphasizes that effective AI use requires "investing" tokens in meaningful tasks rather than merely spending them, and that users must build robust verification structures to trust their agents, similar to how professional credentials establish trust in other fields.
Ultimately, the goal is to achieve a state of high trust and high cognitive depth, where users leverage AI to augment their own skills and create novel solutions rather than relying on it for basic recall.
