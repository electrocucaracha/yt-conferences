# Summary

Justin Schrader argues that the current "line shaft" era of AI, where a single large model powers diverse tasks, is inefficient and risky, drawing a parallel to pre-electric factory systems.
He proposes a shift toward domain-specific agents—small, specialized harnesses built for one or two specific tasks—which offer superior integration, trust, and efficacy compared to general-purpose models or simple MCP skills.
This approach allows businesses to leverage the "jaggedness" of different models, using cheaper, specialized models for specific tasks to reduce costs and improve reliability, rather than relying on untrustworthy public benchmarks.
Schrader emphasizes that the true unlock for this paradigm is the establishment of industry standards that make agents portable, vendor-agnostic, and composable, similar to how electrical standards revolutionized manufacturing.
His company, Standard Agents, aims to create an open-source specification and runtime for these agents, enabling a future where businesses can install, govern, and maintain specialized AI workflows with guaranteed performance and ease of deployment.
