# Summary

Katherine Druckman, who leads community strategy at JetBrains, argues that the open source ecosystem’s reliance on volunteer maintainers is increasingly unsustainable, particularly as AI accelerates code generation without proportionally increasing review capacity.
She highlights that despite critical incidents like Heartbleed and Log4Shell, core projects remain underfunded, with data showing that nearly 70% of popular npm packages still rely on a single maintainer.
Druckman contends that AI has shifted the burden of contribution from writing code to reviewing and understanding changes, creating a "high-quality chaos" that overwhelms small teams, as evidenced by the struggles of the cURL project and Anthropic’s vulnerability disclosure pipeline.
She emphasizes that "human in the loop" is not merely a safety feature but a critical resource that requires proper support, funding, and clear accountability, especially when AI agents select dependencies that become the user’s responsibility.
To address these challenges, she urges organizations to budget for review and mentoring, assign specific owners to critical dependencies, and ensure that AI-assisted contributions are accompanied by genuine engagement and follow-through to maintain the health of the software supply chain.
