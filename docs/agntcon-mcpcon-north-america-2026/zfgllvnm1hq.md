# Summary

Jake, who leads membership development at the Agentic AI Foundation (AIF), explains the foundation’s governance structure, which includes a governing board, a technical committee, and various working groups that oversee ten distinct projects.
He highlights significant representation gaps, noting that technology vendors hold 76% of all 33 governance seats, while end users, universities, and nonprofits hold only 10% and 0% respectively, despite the latter being able to participate for free.
This imbalance means that standards are largely written by vendors, potentially forcing unrelated industries and regions like Europe, India, and Latin America to conform to protocols that do not reflect their specific needs or regulatory environments.
To address this, Jake emphasizes that influence is earned through consistent participation, such as attending working group meetings to count toward quorum, proposing agenda items, and eventually seeking leadership roles.
He encourages individuals and organizations from diverse sectors to actively join working groups, contribute to code, and engage in community chapters to ensure that the evolving agentic AI standards are shaped by a broad and inclusive set of stakeholders rather than just a vendor-led minority.
