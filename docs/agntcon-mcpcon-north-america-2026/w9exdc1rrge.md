# Summary

The speaker argues that while AI has democratized the ability to build software, it simultaneously creates a risk of homogenization because models generate average outputs based on historical data.
Consequently, the primary differentiator for creators is no longer technical implementation, which is becoming commoditized, but rather the ability to identify and solve genuinely important, specific problems derived from personal experience and real-world needs.
Drawing on Richard Hamming’s insights, the speaker emphasizes that having an "attack" on a significant problem is crucial, and in the age of AI, the rarest asset is the unique human perspective that identifies these non-obvious opportunities.
Furthermore, the speaker warns that even a strong product signal can be distorted during go-to-market efforts if messaging relies on generic AI-generated language, which can obscure the product’s actual value and scope.
To succeed, builders must protect their unique signal by defining clear, honest promises and ensuring their communication accurately reflects their specific solution, thereby building trust in a market saturated with average, interchangeable tools.
