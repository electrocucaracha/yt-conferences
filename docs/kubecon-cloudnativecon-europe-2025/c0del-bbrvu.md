# Summary

Colin Murphy, a senior Rust engineer at Adobe, shares his experience with Spiffy, a workload identity solution for WebAssembly.
He recounts how he was tasked with implementing security scans for critical vulnerabilities in their FedRamp environment, which led him to explore alternative solutions.
Colin discovered Spiffy and its potential to replace Docker and Kubernetes for workload identity.
He highlights the benefits of using Spiffy, including its ability to provide secure, sandboxed environments for untrusted code, efficient execution, and global scalability.
The presentation showcases a proof-of-concept demo that demonstrates how Spiffy can be used with WebAssembly components to acquire an SVID (Spiffy Identity) token, which can then be used to authenticate with AWS services like Bedrock.
Colin emphasizes the importance of workload identity in the age of AI and machine learning, where secure decision-making is crucial.
