# Summary

The speaker, a maintainer of the Containerd project, discusses the challenges of sustaining open source projects that are over ten years old, noting that while the project remains healthy, it has benefited from strategic governance changes rather than just luck.
He highlights the introduction of new roles, such as reviewers, security advisors, and maintainer emeritus, which have helped create a clearer contributor ladder, provided a "cooling off" period for new members, and relieved voting bottlenecks caused by inactive maintainers.
Despite these successes, the speaker acknowledges ongoing struggles with bug management for newbies, documentation upkeep, and the lack of full-time contributors, which impacts responsiveness.
He recommends fostering tangible community connections through Slack, regular calls, and in-person events, while also emphasizing the importance of asynchronous efficiency and rotating roles to ensure knowledge distribution.
The session concludes with a Q&A addressing how to handle unresponsive new contributors, the value of direct mentorship, and the specific approach Containerd takes to integrating security experts who may not be core code contributors.
