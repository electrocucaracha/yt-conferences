# Summary

A group of current and former members of the Kubernetes and CNCF Code of Conduct committees, including Hillary Lipig, Danielle, Jeremy, Margarita Medina, and Andrew Javinsky, present a talk on the critical distinction between intent and impact in open source communication.
They explain that while intent refers to the meaning a speaker intends to convey, impact is how the message is received, noting that well-meaning phrases like "assume good intent" can sometimes be weaponized to dismiss valid concerns about inconsiderate behavior.
To mitigate misunderstandings arising from text-based interactions, the speakers advise slowing down to avoid impulsive responses, recognizing that immediate replies are rarely necessary in open source contexts.
They emphasize the importance of cultural awareness and intentionality, suggesting that individuals should explicitly state their intent to seek understanding rather than appear belligerent, and lead with curiosity to bridge perspective gaps.
Finally, the presentation introduces the "talk with your ear" strategy, which involves approaching conflicts with empathy, respect, and attention by acknowledging others' feelings, validating their efforts, and taking time to reflect before responding to ensure constructive dialogue.
