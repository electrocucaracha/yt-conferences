# Summary

Mark Gibbons delivers a talk at PyCon US titled "Finding 2.0," using the concept of software versioning as a metaphor for personal growth and life transformation.
He begins by noting that nearly 90% of Python packages remain below version 2.0, attributing this hesitation to a fear of imperfection and imposter syndrome, which he argues is a cultural reluctance to embrace breaking changes.
Gibbons shares his personal journey, detailing his transition from a professional musician to a software developer, followed by a severe battle with Hodgkin’s lymphoma and a subsequent struggle with alcohol addiction.
He describes his decision to undergo a "rewrite" of his life, or "Mark 2.0," by setting a clear intention, conducting a self-assessment, defining a vision, accepting the need for change, and creating a structured plan across health, family, community, career, and finances.
The speaker emphasizes that major version bumps are not sacred but are necessary signals of significant, backward-incompatible changes, urging the audience to proactively design their own growth rather than waiting for disaster to force it.
He concludes by encouraging developers to apply these principles to both their codebases and their lives, asserting that within hardship lies the opportunity for profound transformation.
