# Summary

Jacob Coffee, a new infrastructure engineer at the Python Software Foundation (PSF), shares insights from his first year of service, highlighting the organization’s mission to promote the Python language and support a diverse global community.
He details the PSF’s operations, including managing over $3 million in grants, organizing PyCon US, and maintaining critical infrastructure like PyPI and CPython build bots, which are supported by a small staff, strategic partners like Fastly and AWS, and a vast network of volunteers.
Coffee emphasizes the critical role of volunteers and sponsors in sustaining these services, noting that the infrastructure was previously maintained largely by a single staff member before his arrival.
He also discusses his personal contributions, such as joining the CPython triage team and participating in GitHub Universe, while encouraging the audience to engage with the community through various work groups, regional meetups, and open-source contributions.
The talk concludes with an invitation for attendees to participate in upcoming sprints and events, reinforcing the collaborative spirit that drives the Python ecosystem forward.
