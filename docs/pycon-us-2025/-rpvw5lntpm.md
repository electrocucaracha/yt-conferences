# Summary

Jack Linky, a US Marine Corps officer and self-described "Excel nerd," shares his journey of building a water management platform for irrigation districts using Python and Django.
Starting with a request to improve a paper-based system in Arizona, he realized that spreadsheets were insufficient for handling complex water allocation logic, data integrity, and time-bound resource distribution.
Despite having no formal computer science background, he chose Python and Django largely due to the supportive and inclusive nature of the community, which helped him navigate technical challenges like modeling water networks as directed acyclic graphs.
Linky emphasizes the importance of keeping solutions simple, avoiding unnecessary complexity like microservices or heavy frontend frameworks, and leveraging tools like HTMX to maintain efficiency.
He also highlights the personal challenges he faced, including imposter syndrome, ADHD, and balancing two full-time jobs, advocating for vulnerability, documentation, and embracing the messy process of learning.
His talk concludes with an encouraging message that anyone, regardless of their background, can build meaningful software by focusing on the problem, sticking with the community, and not waiting for perfect qualifications to start.
