# Summary

Dr.
Carrie L.
Jordan, Executive Director of The Carpentries, delivered the final keynote at PyCon US 2025, emphasizing that technology must be rooted in justice, equity, and care rather than existing solely for technical advancement.
She highlighted the importance of inclusive communities, sharing her personal experiences with the National Society of Black Engineers and the Zumba instructor network to illustrate how belonging and collaboration drive professional success and self-efficacy.
Jordan detailed The Carpentries’ mission to democratize data and coding skills, citing their global impact of training over 5,100 instructors and impacting more than 100,000 new programmers across 71 countries.
She showcased specific community-curated Python lessons focused on social impact, such as tools for agricultural research, public health, and environmental sustainability, while urging developers to challenge rigid educational cultures that resist change and exclude marginalized voices.
By highlighting community members who use Python to automate tedious tasks and improve fairness in machine learning, she argued that building software is a movement for social good that requires active collaboration, transparency, and a commitment to amplifying underrepresented perspectives.
Jordan concluded with a call to action for the Python community to teach, contribute to projects grounded in social good, and amplify each other’s stories to ensure technology serves equity rather than exclusion.
