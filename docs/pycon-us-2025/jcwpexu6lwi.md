# Summary

Dawn Cooper, an accidental accessibility advocate, delivers a talk on web accessibility at PyCon, beginning with a personal anecdote about her flight to Pittsburgh and dedicating the session to her late friend, Annie Braxton.
She defines disability and accessibility through the social model, arguing that the environment, not the individual, must be adapted to remove barriers.
Cooper illustrates common design failures with case studies involving Atlassian Confluence, Domino’s Pizza, and League of Legends, highlighting issues such as inaccessible pop-ups, lack of screen reader support, and color blindness challenges.
She emphasizes the critical importance of avoiding flashing effects that can trigger photosensitive epilepsy and discusses the security vulnerabilities of medical devices like insulin pumps, which pose direct risks to users’ lives.
The presentation also covers the inadequacies of AI-generated captions and the necessity of human-reviewed transcripts for deaf and hard-of-hearing audiences.
Cooper concludes by stressing that accessible design is good design, urging developers to integrate accessibility from the start, conduct thorough testing, and actively include disabled people in the development process to ensure technology serves everyone.
