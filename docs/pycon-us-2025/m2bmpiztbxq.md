# Summary

Laura, an artist and data visualization expert, presents a talk on how color theory influences data visualization, arguing that color perception is not objective but is interpreted by the brain based on context, lighting, and adjacent colors.
She references Joseph Albers’ work to demonstrate that colors can deceive the eye, using optical illusions to show how identical hues appear different depending on their surroundings.
To create honest and accurate infographics, she advises balancing two key concepts: color weight (the darkness or heaviness of a hue) and color temperature (the perceived warmth or coolness of a color).
She explains that unbalanced palettes, common in graphic design, can introduce bias into data charts, making some data points appear more prominent than others; therefore, designers should adjust color weights and temperatures to ensure even visual impact.
Practical strategies include using color spaces like HSL or OKCH for intuitive adjustments, utilizing contrast checkers to minimize differences between adjacent colors, and testing palettes in black and white to verify weight balance.
Finally, she encourages practitioners to view themselves as artists who communicate visually, recommending resources such as Fritz Horstman’s tutorials and interactive digital tools to practice these techniques and develop a more nuanced understanding of color in data representation.
