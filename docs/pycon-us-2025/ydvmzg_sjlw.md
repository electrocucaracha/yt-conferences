# Summary

The speaker introduces the concept of "enshitification," a three-stage process where digital platforms decay by first locking in users, then degrading service to enrich business customers, and finally extracting all remaining value, exemplified by Google’s deliberate reduction of search accuracy to increase ad revenue.
This decay is enabled by "twiddling," a technical mechanism allowing real-time manipulation of business logic, such as nursing apps lowering wages based on a nurse’s debt load, which harms both workers and patients.
The speaker argues that this phenomenon is not inevitable but results from specific policy failures, including the dismantling of antitrust enforcement, regulatory capture, and the passage of anti-interoperability laws like the DMCA that criminalize reverse engineering and repair.
To counter this, the speaker advocates for a global resurgence in antitrust vigor, the repeal of anti-circumvention laws to enable interoperability and right-to-repair, and the restoration of worker power, asserting that these policy changes can create a hostile environment for exploitative corporate practices.
Ultimately, the talk concludes that while the current digital landscape is a product of deliberate, reversible policy choices, building a "new good internet" requires collective action to dismantle these structural barriers and restore user agency.
