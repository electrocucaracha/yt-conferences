# Summary

Rodrigo shares personal lessons from his 503 days working full-time on free and open source software, emphasizing that these experiences are subjective and not necessarily unique to FOSS.
He highlights that maintaining an active online presence serves as publicity that can lead to job opportunities, while also stressing the importance of managing one’s ego by embracing code reviews and asking questions to learn from more experienced colleagues.
A central takeaway is the necessity of creating a detailed contributing guide, which helps standardize bug reports, clarify style guidelines, and facilitate respectful interactions with users and external contributors.
Rodrigo advises being exceptionally kind in text-based communication to avoid misunderstandings and recommends responding quickly to new issues and pull requests to maintain community engagement.
Finally, he discusses strategies for handling large codebases, such as focusing on the spirit of issues rather than just their literal text, and suggests reviewing one’s own draft pull requests before submission to ensure quality.
