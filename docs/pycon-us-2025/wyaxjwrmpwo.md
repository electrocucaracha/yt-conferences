# Summary

Natalia and Rocío Sarmiento, researchers at the National University of the South in Argentina, presented their interdisciplinary experience using Python for geomonitoring and environmental analysis in Latin America.
Over two decades, their work evolved from proprietary tools like MATLAB and C++ to a Python-based ecosystem leveraging libraries such as GDAL, NumPy, Rasterio, and Scikit-learn to process satellite imagery, radar data, and video feeds.
Their methodology focuses on decoupling georeferencing from numerical processing to apply machine learning techniques, including semantic segmentation and zero-shot models like SAM, for tasks such as detecting illegal fishing, monitoring coastal erosion, and assessing land-use changes.
A key aspect of their approach is the integration of expert domain knowledge to validate models and address the specific challenges of limited computational infrastructure in the region, allowing for scalable solutions that can be deployed from desktops to cloud environments.
They highlighted recent applications, such as quantifying flood damage in Bahía Blanca by fusing satellite data with meteorological information, emphasizing the importance of open-source code and community collaboration to make scientific results replicable and accessible.
