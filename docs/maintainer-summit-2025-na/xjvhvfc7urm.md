# Summary

This session, led by Anu and Justin, focuses on establishing a formal policy for the use of Generative AI in Kubernetes contributions, moving beyond the previous discussion on running AI workloads.
The core legal challenge identified is that AI agents cannot sign the Contributor License Agreement (CLA), meaning that any AI-assisted code must be submitted by a human who retains full responsibility for the contribution’s legality and quality.
Participants agreed that while AI usage is inevitable and should be permitted, the human contributor remains liable for ensuring the code does not infringe on copyrights or violate project standards, effectively treating AI as a tool rather than an independent author.
The discussion also explored the boundaries of AI autonomy, concluding that while AI bots could potentially handle low-risk tasks like typo fixes or test generation, they must build trust over time and likely require human oversight for approvals or merges.
The group reached a consensus that the current de facto policy—where humans are responsible for AI-assisted contributions under the existing CLA—should be formalized, with next steps including drafting a specific policy document for CNCF feedback and seeking legal clarification from the Linux Foundation to address potential copyright risks.
