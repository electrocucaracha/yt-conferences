# Summary

Brent Keller and John Shell present a discussion on "conference-driven development," proposing that CNCF events like KubeCon should encourage speakers to prototype and build new features or integrations in real-time between submission and the conference date.
They argue that this approach fosters innovation by allowing maintainers and contributors to pursue ambitious, bleeding-edge ideas that might otherwise remain unaddressed due to lack of time or resources.
The speakers address potential risks, such as the possibility of failure or security concerns with unproven code, by emphasizing a culture where learning from negative results is valuable and by suggesting that integrating existing open-source projects is a safer and more impactful way to demonstrate new capabilities.
They also highlight the importance of balancing advanced, experimental topics with introductory sessions to accommodate both new attendees and experienced experts, while noting that program committees must carefully evaluate submissions to distinguish between genuine innovation and vendor pitches.
Ultimately, the presentation advocates for creating more space for dynamic, community-driven development within conference tracks, encouraging attendees to view conference talks as catalysts for building new tools and solving complex problems collaboratively.
