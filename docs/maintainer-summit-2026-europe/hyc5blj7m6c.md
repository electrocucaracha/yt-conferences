# Summary

The Kubernetes Steering Committee addressed the significant burden of low-quality, AI-generated pull requests, which have increased reviewer workload by approximately tenfold.
To mitigate this, the committee is updating its AI policy to mandate the disclosure of AI usage and strictly prohibiting AI agents from co-authoring commits or signing Contributor License Agreements, a stance supported by the Linux Foundation’s legal team.
The committee explicitly rejected proposals to integrate AI tools for steering decisions or to allow "assisted by" commit signatures, emphasizing that human accountability and respectful community interaction are paramount.
While acknowledging the legal uncertainties surrounding AI-generated content and copyright, the committee delegated the evaluation of specific AI tools to individual SIGs and subprojects, refusing to create a centralized whitelist or blacklist.
Finally, the committee highlighted the importance of human-centric governance, noting that while AI presents new challenges, the project remains committed to protecting contributor rights and maintaining high-quality, human-driven development processes.
