# Summary

As a Site Reliability Engineer at Google, Jason Kahu discusses chaos engineering, a practice of intentionally disrupting systems to test disaster recovery plans.
Chaos engineering began in 2006 as "disaster resiliency testing" (Dirt) and has since grown into a company-wide initiative to test emergency responses, procedures, and even building maintenance.
The goal is to discover problems in controlled circumstances before they occur naturally, allowing teams to develop muscle memory for responding to incidents.
Google measures the effectiveness of Dirt tests by analyzing results and finding that teams that participate more heavily in these exercises have shorter incident response times.
