# Summary

Mikhail Pavlikowski, author of the Chaos Engineering book, argues that making chaos engineering "boring" is a good thing, as it becomes part of everyday life.
He defines chaos engineering as experimenting on a system to build confidence in its ability to withstand turbulent conditions.
Pavlikowski debunks common myths about chaos engineering, including the notion that it's only for large organizations or that it requires extensive training.
Instead, he emphasizes the importance of risk and reward analysis, using the "shark and hamburger paradox" to illustrate how people overestimate the danger of shark attacks while underestimating the risks of everyday activities like eating a hamburger.
Pavlikowski concludes by promoting his book as a practical guide to chaos engineering, covering topics from building and testing legacy applications to working with Kubernetes.
