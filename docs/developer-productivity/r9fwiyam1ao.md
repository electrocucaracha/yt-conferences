# Summary

The speaker reflects on their journey from learning object-oriented programming (OOP) concepts as a young developer to realizing its limitations.
They argue that OOP's focus on sending messages between objects, rather than modeling processes, leads to transactional thinking and brittleness.
The speaker identifies the "transactional fallacy" as a common problem in software development, where processes are modeled as transactions, leading to issues like brittle code and failure to adapt to surprises.
They propose adopting a narrative perspective, focusing on direction and becoming, rather than goal-oriented thinking, and embracing interdependence and empathy in code.
