# Summary

Kevlin Henney is in conversation with James Lewis, technical director at Thoughtworks, discussing complexity science and its application to software architecture.
They explore the concept of generative science, which studies complex systems that arise from simplicity, and how it relates to microservices and team topologies.
James highlights the importance of acknowledging sociotechnical aspects, such as people and workflow, in software architecture, rather than just focusing on technical complexity.
He also emphasizes the need for a richer vocabulary and diagrams that represent the actual domain being worked in, rather than just technology-centric ones.
The conversation touches on topics like fractal dimensions, self-similarity, and the idea that companies can be seen as examples of general artificial intelligence.
