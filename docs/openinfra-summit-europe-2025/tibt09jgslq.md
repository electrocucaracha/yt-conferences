# Summary

At the Open Infrastructure Summit, speakers Samashki and Kalof presented on the critical synergy between digital sovereignty and open source, emphasizing that openness empowers nations to control their digital futures.
They introduced the GStack initiative, a vendor-neutral framework of specifications that allows countries to procure and build public services without being locked into proprietary ecosystems, aligning with United Nations goals for democratic digitalization.
The presentation detailed the Sovereign Cloud Stack, a reference implementation built on OpenStack and Kubernetes, which addresses four key levels of sovereignty: data control, provider choice, technological understanding, and operational knowledge.
To support this, the team developed certifiable standards and open-source training materials, such as those created for workshops in Kenya, to ensure that even smaller nations can maintain their infrastructure independently.
Ultimately, the speakers argued that digital sovereignty and open source are mutually dependent, enabling countries like Kazakhstan to achieve high levels of digital service availability and contribute back to the global open-source community.
