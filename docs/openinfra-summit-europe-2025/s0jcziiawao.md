# Summary

Wong from China Mobile presents the next-generation computing power network, a new infrastructure proposed by China that integrates computing, data, and security to make computing power a social-level service similar to water and electricity.
China Mobile has established the world’s largest such network, featuring two super computing centers, 13 regional nodes, and high-speed direct connections that enable low-latency services for applications like digital humans, cloud gaming, and AI agents.
The presentation details the network’s architecture, which layers core, edge, and terminal computing over IP and optical networks, supported by an intelligent orchestration platform that achieves autonomous operation and maintenance.
China Mobile actively contributes to the open-source community through the CFN working group, which includes 19 members and focuses on key areas such as ubiquitous computing scheduling, computing-native software stacks, and heterogeneous distributed training.
Specific technical efforts include developing unified programming models for cross-architecture AI migration, optimizing hybrid training across different AI chips, and providing integrated computing and network scheduling solutions for mobile services like autonomous driving and XR.
