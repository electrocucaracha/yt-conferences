# Summary

The speaker, a member of the OpenStack Foundation governing board, presents a forum session to gather community feedback on the adoption of the foundation’s AI-assisted software development policy.
He explains that the policy was introduced to address a shrinking contributor base by leveraging AI tools to reduce mechanical toil, but initial adoption was low due to concerns that the "generated by" label implied excessive tool agency and the policy text was overly complex.
Following community feedback, the board revised the policy to include an "assisted by" label and simplified the language, which resulted in a noticeable increase in usage, though the total number of AI-assisted patches remains a small fraction of overall contributions.
The discussion highlights that commercial tools like Claude Code dominate usage over open-source alternatives, which are perceived as less effective, and identifies cultural barriers, such as fear of reputational damage, as significant impediments to broader adoption.
Participants suggest that visible leadership endorsement, better communication of the policy’s existence, and tracking rejected AI-assisted patches to identify quality issues are crucial next steps to normalize AI usage and improve community health.
