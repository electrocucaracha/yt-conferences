# Summary

This panel discussion, moderated by Jonathan Bryce of the Open Foundation, explores the transformative impact of AI on telecommunications and network infrastructure.
Panelists from Omdia, Verizon, and Huawei emphasize that while AI is a major driver of change, it operates alongside other critical shifts such as the rollout of 5G, preparation for 6G, and the convergence of wireless, satellite, and edge technologies.
A key insight shared is that "AI workloads" are often better defined as large-scale, distributed data processing tasks that require moving data between data centers, private clouds, and the edge, rather than just generative AI applications.
The discussion highlights specific operational improvements, such as Verizon reducing circuit setup time from days to minutes through machine learning automation, and notes that while large language models are popular, specialized small language models and traditional machine learning are more frequently used for network optimization, fault identification, and resource management.
Looking ahead, the panel predicts that AI workloads will increasingly move to the edge to meet low-latency requirements for applications like autonomous vehicles and robotic surgery, necessitating unified infrastructure platforms that can manage diverse workloads across CPU, GPU, and NPU resources.
Finally, the experts agree that while AI will automate many processes, physical tasks such as laying fiber and maintaining hardware will still require human labor, and the industry must continue to innovate to find practical, high-value use cases that benefit both operators and end users.
