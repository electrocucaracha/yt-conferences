# Summary

Armstrong Fam from Polytechnique and the Zil project presents a case study on leveraging OpenStack to power large-scale AI research, specifically highlighting its role in supporting major Canadian institutions like Compute Canada and Mila.
The talk emphasizes that AI requires rigorous software engineering practices to ensure stability, using the critical question of whether an AI agent can safely take over control from a human pilot as a central research driver.
Motivated by the Boeing 737 MAX crashes and the dangers of single points of failure, the team developed robust systems for safe landing and takeoff by training models on diverse runway data to handle out-of-distribution scenarios and difficult weather conditions.
The presentation underscores the necessity of explainability in safety-critical systems, arguing that AI decisions must be transparent to prevent bias and ensure fairness, as illustrated by real-world examples of unjustified automated rejections.
Finally, the speaker discusses the importance of adversarial robustness, demonstrating how attackers can manipulate code comments to alter AI-generated outcomes, and advocates for open-sourcing these libraries to allow the community to identify and mitigate such vulnerabilities for the benefit of humanity.
