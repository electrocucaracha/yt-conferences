# Summary

This forum session explores the ambiguity of the term "sovereignty" within the OpenStack community, highlighting how different stakeholders interpret it through the lenses of data protection, technological independence, and operational control.
Participants argue that true sovereignty is not a static attribute of a cloud provider but a dynamic capability for users to manage risk, exit providers, and maintain control over their infrastructure, particularly in contrast to hyperscalers who may be subject to extraterritorial laws.
The discussion emphasizes that while OpenStack provides the necessary open-source foundation for achieving these goals, significant challenges remain regarding interoperability, API standardization, and the lack of a unified definition that distinguishes between regulatory compliance and genuine user autonomy.
To address these issues, the group proposes creating a special interest group to draft a white paper that defines a common taxonomy of sovereignty requirements, analyzes regional regulatory frameworks, and establishes best practices for achieving resilience and portability.
Ultimately, the consensus is that the community must move beyond vague terminology to create concrete technical standards and collaborative frameworks that enable users to verify and achieve their specific sovereignty needs.
